1 2 Marks Salon — personalised haircut based on face shape & lifestyle Which characteristic of services is highlighted?

Characteristic: Variability (Heterogeneity) — also acceptable: Inseparability.

Justification: Services vary by the person providing them, the customer receiving them, and the context. A haircut is unique to each customer's face/lifestyle — no two services are identical. The stylist personalises the experience because services cannot be standardised the way physical products can.

2 2 Marks Apparel — "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" during festivals Type of sales promotion + one short-term benefit.

Type: Sales Promotion — BOGO (Buy One Get One), a consumer-facing offer.

Short-term Benefit: Generates an immediate sales spike, helps clear unsold inventory, and attracts price-sensitive shoppers during festivals — boosting cash flow and brand visibility during peak retail seasons.

3 2 Marks Nutritional supplement — distributors earn from own sales + downline recruits Identify the distribution model.

Model: Multi-Level Marketing (MLM) / Network Marketing.

Explanation: MLM is a distribution model where distributors earn commissions both from their own sales and from the sales of new distributors they recruit (their "downline"). This creates a multi-layered network. Examples: Amway, Herbalife, Modicare. Distinct from pyramid schemes when commissions are genuinely tied to product sales, not just recruitment.

4 2 Marks Coffee chain — consistent taste, ambience, service across all outlets Which extended P is being emphasised?

Extended P: Process — supported by People and Physical Evidence.

Justification: Process refers to the standardised systems and procedures of service delivery (recipes, training manuals, billing flow, store layout, hygiene SOPs). Coffee chains like Starbucks invest heavily in process standardisation to ensure that every outlet — regardless of city — delivers a consistent, predictable customer experience. This reduces service variability, the biggest challenge for service businesses.

5 2 Marks Entrepreneur — online survey of 500 customers pre-launch Type of research?

Type: Primary Quantitative Research (specifically, a Survey).

Explanation: Surveys collect firsthand data (hence Primary) using structured questions to measure and quantify customer preferences, behaviour or attitudes (hence Quantitative). They are fast, cost-effective and statistically representative when designed correctly — ideal for pre-launch validation.

7 3 Marks Clothing brand pre-launch — SWOT (a) Explain SWOT. (b) Give one Strength & Threat for Indian clothing brand entering modern retail.

(a) SWOT Framework:

  • Strengths (Internal +): What the company does better than competitors.
  • Weaknesses (Internal −): Where the company is vulnerable.
  • Opportunities (External +): Favourable trends the company can exploit.
  • Threats (External −): External factors that could damage the business.

SWOT is a diagnostic tool — it doesn't tell you what to do, but what to think about before deciding.

(b) Indian Clothing Brand entering Modern Retail:

  • Strength: Deep understanding of Indian body types, cultural preferences, festive collection design — gives competitive advantage against generic global brands.
  • Threat: Entry of global fast-fashion brands like Zara, H&M, Shein who offer trendy designs at competitive prices with global brand pull.
8 3 Marks Bakery — frequent discounts but no repeat purchase growth Three risks of over-promotion + one alternative.

Three Risks of Over-Promotion:

  1. Brand Dilution: Constant discounts signal "low value" — customers stop perceiving the brand as premium or quality.
  2. Deal Addiction: Customers wait for offers and refuse to pay full price. Repeat purchases at full price drop.
  3. Margin Erosion: Discounting eats into profitability without building lasting loyalty.

Alternative Strategy:

Build a Loyalty Programme + Brand Experience: Instead of constant discounts, introduce a tiered loyalty programme (e.g., earn points → redeem for free pastries). Combine this with experiential touches — birthday cakes, personalised messages, premium packaging. This creates emotional connection, encourages repeat purchase, and supports full-price selling.

9 3 Marks Premium watch e-commerce — media mix choice Recommend media + justify via reach × frequency × impact.

Recommendation: Airport Hoardings + Premium Instagram Influencer Marketing (skip TV).

Justification using R × F × I:

DimensionStrategic Choice
ReachNarrow but targeted. Airport audiences = wealthy travellers. Premium Instagram followers = aspirational youth. No need for mass reach (TV) which dilutes brand.
FrequencyModerate. Hoardings hit the same audience multiple times during travel cycles. Influencer posts can be repeated subtly.

Why NOT TV? TV is broad and cheap-mass — it would dilute premium positioning. Luxury brand media must match the brand experience.

10 9 Marks Case Study — Amazon Prime's Subscription Loyalty
Context Recap: Amazon Prime (since 2016 in India) bundles free fast delivery + Prime Video + Music + exclusive deals into one annual subscription. Members spend 2–3× more than non-members. Personalised recommendations + exclusive sale access.

(a) Type of Loyalty Programme + Two Benefits (4 Marks):

Type: Paid Subscription Loyalty Programme (also known as Premium Membership). Customers pay an annual fee for exclusive benefits.

Two Benefits to Customers:

  1. Bundled Value (Multi-Service Access): One subscription unlocks fast free delivery + video streaming (Prime Video) + music (Prime Music) + exclusive sales access (Prime Day). The perceived bundled value far exceeds the subscription fee.
  2. Convenience & Exclusivity: Members enjoy faster delivery, early sale access, and priority customer service — making the shopping experience smoother and more rewarding.

(b) Bundling, Personalisation & CLV (5 Marks):

  1. Bundling Creates "Pay Once, Use Often" Mentality: Once subscribed, members feel obliged to "get their money's worth" → more orders, more streaming → deeper habit formation.
  2. Personalisation Drives Relevance: Amazon's recommendation engine uses purchase + browsing + Prime Video viewing data to suggest hyper-relevant products. Higher relevance = higher conversion.
  3. CLV Calculation Logic:
    • CLV = Average Order Value × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan.
    • Prime members buy 2–3× more frequently → Higher CLV.
    • Lifespan is longer because of stickiness — they don't churn easily.
  4. Cross-Selling Across Services: Prime Video viewers see ads for Prime-eligible products. Music listeners get pushed exclusive deals. Each service feeds the others — a flywheel effect.
  5. Retention & Lock-In: Annual subscription model creates an automatic 1-year retention window. Renewal rates remain high because cancellation feels like loss of multiple services at once.
Conclusion: Amazon Prime is a textbook example of how bundling + personalisation + CLV-focused thinking turns customers into long-term, high-value users — and creates an ecosystem competitors struggle to match.
11 9 Marks Case Study — Swiggy Instamart vs Local Kirana
Context Recap: Swiggy Instamart promises 10–15 min grocery delivery using neighbourhood dark stores, dynamic pricing & offers. Local kiranas losing footfall; modern retailers responding with their own quick-commerce.

(a) Two Marketing Mix Elements giving Instamart Advantage (4 Marks):

  1. Place (Distribution): Instamart's network of dark stores located within neighbourhoods enables 10-minute delivery. This hyper-local infrastructure creates time utility — instant gratification — which traditional retail cannot match. Place is Instamart's biggest competitive advantage.
  2. Price + Promotion (Dynamic Pricing & Offers): Aggressive discounts, free-delivery for first orders, and surge-free promises make Instamart attractive to price-sensitive yet convenience-seeking customers. Promotions drive trial → habit formation.

(Bonus: Product — curated SKUs optimised for fast delivery; Process — fast picking, payment integration with UPI.)

(b) Impact on Kiranas + Two Strategies for them (5 Marks):

Impact:

  • Loss of footfall, especially among urban young consumers who value speed & convenience.
  • Reduced revenue on packaged/commodity items where price difference matters more.
  • However, kiranas still hold strong on credit relationships, personal trust, fresh produce, and last-second small purchases.

Two Strategies for Kiranas to Stay Competitive:

  1. Embrace Hyperlocal Tech (Partner with Apps like DotPe, Lokal, WhatsApp Business): Offer WhatsApp ordering, mobile payments (UPI), home delivery within 30 minutes via local Rapido/Dunzo partnerships. Combine kirana relationship with app convenience.
  2. Differentiate on Service + Niche: Focus on fresh produce, personalised recommendations, credit (udhaar khata), bulk & bulk-break (loose flour, oil), and emotional connection — areas where quick commerce cannot easily compete. Become the trusted local advisor, not just a transactional shop.
Conclusion: Quick commerce is reshaping urban grocery but kirana's deep customer relationships + trust remain irreplaceable. The winners will be kiranas who blend tradition with technology, not those who try to compete head-on on speed alone.
12 9 Marks Case Study — Boat Lifestyle's Influencer-Driven Growth
Context Recap: Boat sells affordable wireless audio products priced 30–50% lower than international brands. Uses micro-influencers, cricketers, YouTubers (not celebrities). Now top-selling earwear brand in India, competing with Sony, JBL, Apple.

(a) Segmentation & Positioning Approach (4 Marks):

Segmentation Bases:

  • Demographic: Young Indians aged 16–30, students & early-career professionals.
  • Psychographic: Music lovers, gamers, fitness enthusiasts, value-for-money seekers, brand-aware but budget-conscious.
  • Behavioural: Active social media users, content consumers, frequent earphone users.

Positioning Statement:

"For young India that loves music, gaming, and fitness — Boat offers premium-looking, affordable audio that fits your style and budget."

Boat positioned itself as "affordable premium" — the look & feel of expensive brands at one-third the price. Strong colour design language reinforces aspiration.

(b) Digital Promotion + Influencer Marketing + Pricing — How Boat Differentiated (5 Marks):

  1. Digital-First Promotion: Instead of TV ads, Boat focused on Instagram Reels, YouTube reviews, and content marketing — channels where its target audience (Gen Z) spends time. This is highly cost-effective vs traditional media.
  2. Micro-Influencer Army: Hundreds of small-to-mid sized influencers (1K–500K followers) created authentic reviews — feels organic, not paid celebrity endorsements. Builds trust + reach at low cost per impression.
  3. Strategic Celebrity & Cricket Tie-Ups: Sponsorship of IPL teams, KKR (Kolkata Knight Riders), partnerships with cricketers (Hardik Pandya, Rishabh Pant) — anchored mass aspirational reach without expensive Bollywood superstars.
  4. Penetration Pricing: Products priced 30–50% lower than Sony/JBL/Apple. Made premium audio accessible to college students & young professionals — massively expanding the addressable market.
  5. Product Design Language: Boldly designed (premium-looking colours, finishes) so it doesn't look cheap — reinforces the "affordable premium" positioning visually.
Conclusion: Boat built a billion-rupee brand by recognising that young Indians wanted premium looking and feeling audio at affordable prices — and used new-age digital + influencer marketing instead of expensive traditional media. A textbook case of D2C-meets-segmentation insight.
