10. Case Study – Reliance Jio's Disruptive Entry

When Reliance Jio entered the Indian telecom market in 2016, it offered free 4G data and free voice calls for six months. This aggressive pricing forced competitors to drastically reduce prices, and Jio acquired over 100 million subscribers in less than 6 months. Today, Jio is a market leader and has expanded into fiber broadband, JioMart, and JioCinema.

Case questions:
  1. Identify and explain the pricing strategy and STP approach used by Jio at launch. (4 Marks)
  2. Apply the Ansoff Matrix to explain how Jio is now growing beyond its core telecom business. (5 Marks)

11. Case Study – Maggi's Comeback after the 2015 Crisis

In 2015, Maggi was banned in India due to alleged high lead and MSG content. Nestlé recalled stock worth crores and the brand value plummeted. After re-clearing safety tests, Nestlé re-launched Maggi with new packaging, emotional storytelling ("We missed you too"), heavy promotion through social media, and re-engagement with kirana stores. Within a year, Maggi regained its market leadership.

Case questions:
  1. Identify any two promotional strategies used by Nestlé in Maggi's re-launch and explain their importance. (4 Marks)
  2. Discuss how Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC) helped Maggi rebuild trust and recover its market share. (5 Marks)

12. Case Study – Tata Nano: The Failed Dream

Tata Motors launched the Nano in 2008 as the world's cheapest car at ₹1 lakh, targeting Indian families upgrading from two-wheelers. Despite extensive advertising, the Nano failed in the market. Customers perceived it as a "cheap car", felt it lacked status, and many feared safety issues. Sales never picked up and the Nano was discontinued in 2018.

Case questions:
  1. Identify the marketing mix and STP mistakes that led to Nano's failure. (4 Marks)
  2. Explain the importance of "positioning" and "consumer perception" in product success. How could Tata have repositioned the Nano differently? (5 Marks)
