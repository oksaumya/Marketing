SECTION C — Case-Based Application (2 × 9 = 18 Marks)

Attempt both caselets. Structure your answers with headings, frameworks and labelled diagrams where useful. Suggested length: ~1 page each.

[9 Marks] Q9. Caselet 1 — The "Hyper-Speed" Quick-Commerce War
Context: Three players dominate India's quick-commerce category in 2026 — Zepto (10-minute delivery, dark-store first), Blinkit (owned by Zomato, leveraging restaurant relationships), and Instamart (Swiggy's vertical).

Zepto is growing fast but burning ₹X crores monthly on every delivery. Blinkit has the financial cushion of Zomato. Instamart has the largest customer base but the slowest delivery times.

A new Tier-2 city player, "Apna Bazaar Express", wants to enter the market with a differentiated strategy.
  1. Conduct a brief SWOT analysis for Zepto in this current scenario. (2 marks)
  2. Plot Zepto, Blinkit, and Instamart on the BCG Matrix with justification. Draw a labelled diagram. (3 marks)
  3. Recommend a differentiated 4Ps marketing mix for "Apna Bazaar Express" to compete in Tier-2 cities. (4 marks)
OR
[9 Marks] Q9 (Alt). Caselet 1 (Alternative) — Reviving a Fallen Brand
Context: A mid-sized Indian café chain "Café Mosaic" charges premium prices comparable to Starbucks but suffers from average interiors, slow service, and weak repeat business. Their Instagram ads are bold and aspirational, but customer reviews on Zomato are mixed. Footfall has dropped 30% YoY.
  1. Diagnose which "P" of the marketing mix is the weakest and explain why. (2 marks)
  2. Suggest three concrete actions across the 4Ps to fix the misalignment. Justify each. (4 marks)
  3. Design a simple loyalty program appropriate for Café Mosaic's situation. Specify the program type, earning rule, and one personalisation feature. (3 marks)
[9 Marks] Q10. Caselet 2 — The Streaming Subscription Dilemma
Context: "FlixCine", a regional Indian OTT platform with strong content in Tamil, Telugu and Marathi, has 4 million subscribers but is losing customers to Netflix and Amazon Prime Video. The CMO is debating whether to:

Option A: Cut subscription price by 40% to grow share aggressively (Penetration).
Option B: Add new regional languages & original content at the same price (Product Development).
Option C: Launch a paid premium tier with 4K + early-access movies (Tiered).

Churn data shows 60% of customers leave within 3 months because they "don't see enough new content".
  1. Apply the Ansoff Matrix to place each of the three options (A, B, C) into the correct quadrant. Justify in one line each. (3 marks)
  2. Which option would you recommend and why? Use insights from the churn data and pricing-strategy concepts. (3 marks)
  3. FlixCine wants to retain customers using a loyalty + IMC approach. Suggest:
    1. One loyalty program design (type + key benefit), and
    2. Two consistent IMC touchpoints (e.g. ad, app, email) that reinforce the same message.
    (3 marks)
OR
[9 Marks] Q10 (Alt). Caselet 2 (Alternative) — Distribution Crisis at "Crunchy"
Context: "Crunchy Snacks" is a popular Indian FMCG brand of healthy baked chips. It sells through three channels: (1) Kirana stores (40% of sales), (2) Modern retail like BigBasket & Reliance Smart (35%), and (3) D2C via its own website (25%).

Recently:
  • Modern retailers are demanding 15% higher margins or threatening to delist Crunchy.
  • Kirana stores complain that the company's website sells the same pack at a 20% discount, hurting their walk-ins.
  • The CEO is considering exiting one channel to simplify operations.
  1. Identify the type(s) of channel conflict visible in this case and explain each. (3 marks)
  2. Apply Porter's 5 Forces or any other relevant framework to analyse the bargaining power dynamics at play. (3 marks)
  3. As the marketing head, recommend a channel strategy (with at least three concrete actions) that resolves the conflict without exiting any channel. Justify each. (3 marks)

Section A — Concept Answers

Q1. Need (basic — water), Want (specific — Bisleri), Demand (want + ability to pay — buying a 1L Bisleri for ₹20). Show all 3 + 1 example each = 2 marks.

Q2. STP = Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning. "Segmentation divides, Targeting chooses, Positioning owns the mind."

Q3. 4 I's = Intangibility, Inseparability, Perishability, Variability. Pick one and define properly.

Q4. Penetration = low price for fast adoption (Jio). Skimming = high initial price for early adopters then reduce (iPhone).

Q5. Jio = Cash Cow (mature, high share) · HDFC Savings = Cash Cow · BSNL Landline = Dog · Tata Motors EV = Question Mark / Star (depending on framing).

Section B — Application Answers

Q6. (a) Demographic (18–25 yrs young adults), Psychographic (health-conscious, skincare-aware), Geographic (Tier 1 + Tier 2 metros), Behavioral (skincare routine users). Pick 3. (b) Positioning template — e.g. "For young adults aged 18–25 in metros, our brand is the affordable skincare line that is dermatology-backed and toxin-free, because every product is clinically tested and recommended by certified dermatologists."

Q7. PESTEL for EV India — Political (FAME-II subsidies), Economic (battery costs falling, fuel inflation), Social (climate awareness in urban youth), Technological (improving battery tech, charging infrastructure), Environmental (pollution concerns, climate goals), Legal (BS-VI norms, EV policies of states).

Q8. Influencer unboxing → Interest (and Desire). Limited-edition drop → Desire (scarcity). Free shipping → Action (removes friction). Comparison blog → Interest/Desire (informs evaluation).

Section C — Caselet Skeletons

Q9 (Quick-commerce):

  • SWOT for Zepto — S: speed/focus, W: high burn rate, O: Gen-Z instant gratification, T: Blinkit's deeper pockets.
  • BCG — Zepto = Star (high growth, growing share), Blinkit = Star, Instamart = Question Mark (large base but slower).
  • "Apna Bazaar Express" 4Ps — Product (curated local SKUs, regional brands), Price (slightly higher than kirana but lower than Blinkit, no surge), Place (hub-and-spoke micro-warehouses in Tier-2), Promotion (regional language ads, WhatsApp ordering, local influencer marketing).

Q9 (Alt — Café Mosaic): Weakest P = Product (slow service, average interiors) — but argument can also be made for Place/People. Fixes: improve interiors + staff training (Product), introduce mid-tier pricing for repeat customers (Price), launch app-based ordering + delivery (Place), refresh promotion to feature actual store ambience not just stock photos. Loyalty: Tiered (Silver/Gold) with free upgrade every 5th visit.

Q10 (FlixCine):

  • Ansoff — A = Market Penetration, B = Product Development, C = Product Development (premium tier — same market, new variant).
  • Recommend B — churn is driven by content shortage, not price. Cutting price (A) would hurt margins without solving the real issue. Premium tier (C) is too early before fixing the base offering.
  • Loyalty: Tiered ("FlixGold") with early access + offline meet-ups. IMC touchpoints: WhatsApp content alerts + in-app personalised "Made for You" rail + regional language emails — all carrying the same "Stories made in your language" message.

Q10 (Alt — Crunchy):

  • Vertical conflict — between Crunchy and modern retailers over margins. Multichannel conflict — between Crunchy's D2C site (lower price) and kirana stores.
  • Porter's 5 Forces — buyer power (retailers) very high due to consolidation in modern retail. Channel substitutes (D2C) increasing buyer threat to retailers.
  • Recommendations — (1) Maintain consistent MRP across all channels; offer modern retail loyalty bonuses tied to volume rather than higher margin per unit. (2) Differentiate D2C SKUs (bundles, subscriptions) so they don't directly compete with kirana packs. (3) Co-op marketing fund for kirana stores (POS displays, joint promotions).
