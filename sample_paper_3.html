SECTION C — Case-Based Application (2 × 9 = 18 Marks)

Both compulsory. Use frameworks. ~1 page each.

[9 Marks] Q9. Caselet 1 — Sharmaji ke Samose Goes National
Context: "Sharmaji ke Samose" is a 30-year-old family-owned snack shop in Old Delhi famous for its samosas (₹15 each). Daily footfall: 600 customers. The third-generation owner, Rohan Sharma (MBA grad), wants to take the brand national.

Options he is considering:
  • Open 50 outlets in 10 cities (franchise model)
  • Sell packaged frozen samosas through BigBasket, Amazon Fresh, Blinkit
  • Partner with Swiggy + Zomato for hyperlocal delivery from existing shop
Constraint: He wants to keep the "taste of Old Delhi" intact as the core promise.
  1. Conduct a PESTEL analysis highlighting at least three macro factors that will impact this national expansion. (3 marks)
  2. Conduct a SWOT analysis for Sharmaji ke Samose given the above context. (3 marks)
  3. Which of the three options should Rohan choose to preserve the brand promise while scaling? Justify with reference to positioning and variability/inseparability challenges of services. (3 marks)
OR
[9 Marks] Q9 (Alt). Caselet 1 (Alternative) — The Aspirational Sedan
Context: An Indian carmaker plans to launch a new compact sedan priced between Maruti's mid-range and Hyundai's premium models. The target customer is the "upgrader" — a 30–40 year old urban professional moving up from a hatchback to a sedan. The brand has had mixed brand perception (more value than aspirational).
  1. Develop the STP for this sedan. Be specific about segmentation bases, target description, and positioning statement. (4 marks)
  2. Construct a positioning map with Price (Affordable ↔ Premium) on one axis and Brand Perception (Functional ↔ Aspirational) on the other. Plot at least 5 competitor brands and the new sedan. Draw & label. (3 marks)
  3. Recommend one promotion strategy (message + media + sales promotion) for the launch phase. (2 marks)
[9 Marks] Q10. Caselet 2 — The Big Billion Days Hangover
Context: A mid-tier Indian electronics brand "Voltis" depends heavily on Flipkart Big Billion Days and Amazon Great Indian Sale for 65% of its annual revenue. During sale events they offer 35–50% discounts. Other 9 months: sales are slow.

Findings from a customer survey:
  • 72% customers admit they "wait for sales" before buying Voltis.
  • NPS during full-price months is +12; during sale months it is +45.
  • Repeat purchase rate is only 18%.
  • Their competitor "Boltora" runs only one sale per year and grew 40% YoY.
  1. Diagnose what is happening to Voltis using the short-term vs long-term risks of sales promotion framework. (3 marks)
  2. Should Voltis cut down its sales-event participation? Build the pros and cons and give a recommendation. (3 marks)
  3. Design a brand-building campaign (using IMC principles) that helps Voltis transition from a "discount brand" to a "value brand". Include at least three touchpoints. (3 marks)
OR
[9 Marks] Q10 (Alt). Caselet 2 (Alternative) — The Loyalty Trap
Context: "FlyAway Airlines" runs a tiered frequent-flyer program (Silver, Gold, Platinum) like the Taj InnerCircle. Recently a customer survey revealed:
  • Platinum members fly 2x more than Silver but receive 5x more perks.
  • Silver-tier members feel "ignored" and 35% have shifted to competitors.
  • Acquiring a Platinum customer costs ₹15,000 in marketing; Silver costs ₹2,000.
  • Platinum CLV is ₹3,80,000 over 5 years; Silver CLV is ₹35,000 over 2 years.
  1. Calculate the CLV : CAC ratio for each tier and discuss which tier is more profitable. (3 marks)
  2. Identify two flaws in FlyAway's current loyalty program design and explain how they violate the principle that "loyalty programs should make every customer feel valued". (3 marks)
  3. Redesign the program with two specific changes aimed at retaining Silver members without dilating Platinum benefits. Specify earning rules and one personalisation feature. (3 marks)

Section A

Q1. Value = Benefits − Cost. Two students buy the same ₹399 pizza — one is hungry & gets hot pizza on time (high value), the other isn't hungry & gets cold late delivery (low value). Same price, different value because value is perceived.

Q2. Primary = collected firsthand (Zomato survey before Zomato Gold). Secondary = pre-existing data (Swiggy used FSSAI reports). Qualitative = motivations/feelings (focus groups). Quantitative = measurement (surveys, dashboards).

Q3. Need Recognition → Information Search → Evaluation of Alternatives → Purchase Decision → Post-Purchase. Discounts most influence Purchase Decision. Social media most influences Information Search & Evaluation.

Q4. IMC = coordinating all communication tools so the brand speaks with one voice. Premium-on-Instagram + cheap-on-Amazon breaks trust — customers feel either deceived or confused about real positioning; brand equity drops.

Q5. Three criteria: (1) Is real inventory sold? (2) Are commissions tied to product sales? (3) Is recruitment dominating compensation? MLM = yes/yes/no. Pyramid = no/no/yes.

Section B

Q6. Lifebuoy = Intensive (mass FMCG), Rolex = Exclusive. Premium organic skincare = Selective, Frooti = Intensive. Mass smartphone = Intensive (with selective retail tie-ups), Limited sneakers = Exclusive. Designer Saree = Exclusive, Atta = Intensive.

Q7. Best mix = Airport outdoor + luxury magazines. Reasoning: lower reach but higher impact and ideal context (target audience is wealthy travellers/readers). Mass YouTube/IPL would dilute brand. Google search may help intent but lacks aspirational framing.

Q8. Tabular: Decision speed (B2C fast / B2B slow), Decision maker (one / many), Driver (emotion / rational ROI), Marketing focus (desire / risk reduction), Cycle length (short / long), Channels used (mass / personal selling). Examples: pizza (B2C) vs ERP software (B2B).

Section C — Caselet Skeletons

Q9 (Sharmaji ke Samose):

  • PESTEL: Economic (rising disposable income), Social (snacking culture + nostalgia for "authentic" street food), Tech (food delivery apps, frozen tech), Legal (FSSAI compliance), Environmental (packaging waste from frozen).
  • SWOT — S: brand heritage, distinctive taste, loyal Old Delhi base. W: variability (every shop will taste different), small team, no scale experience. O: pan-India samosa demand, frozen food growth. T: hygiene issues at franchise level, brand dilution, competition from PaperBoat/Haldiram.
  • Recommend: Option 3 (Swiggy/Zomato hyperlocal) FIRST to validate, then frozen via BigBasket (Option 2). Avoid franchises in Option 1 — service variability would destroy the "taste of Old Delhi" promise. Aligns with services' inseparability/variability challenges.

Q9 (Alt — Sedan):

  • STP — Segmentation: Demographic (30–40 yrs, ₹15–25L income), Psychographic (status-conscious upgraders), Geographic (Tier-1 metros). Target: urban professional with first sedan purchase. Positioning: "For young professionals upgrading from hatchbacks, our sedan offers Premium features at a smart-buyer's price."
  • Positioning Map: X = Affordable→Premium, Y = Functional→Aspirational. Maruti = bottom-left, Hyundai = mid-right, Honda = upper-right, Tata = mid-left, BMW = top-right. New sedan = mid-centre (better-than-Maruti, less-than-Honda).
  • Promotion: Message = "Built for the Upgrader". Media = Digital + select print (Auto Today) + experiential test-drives at malls. Sales promo = "Trade-in your hatchback" offer.

Q10 (Voltis):

  • Classic over-promotion trap — short-term spike at the cost of long-term brand equity. Customers anchored to discounted prices, weakening willingness to pay. Boltora's contrarian approach preserved brand value.
  • Yes, cut down to 1–2 sales/year. Pros: rebuilds full-price perception, healthier margins, higher loyalty. Cons: short-term revenue drop, possible market share loss. Mitigation: gradual reduction over 12 months + brand campaign.
  • IMC plan: TV ad showcasing product craftsmanship + Influencer reviews emphasising quality not price + Owned content (YouTube product stories) + PR coverage in tech magazines + targeted retention emails to existing customers. Consistent message: "Worth full price".

Q10 (Alt — FlyAway):

  • CLV:CAC — Platinum = 3,80,000 ÷ 15,000 ≈ 25:1. Silver = 35,000 ÷ 2,000 = 17.5:1. Both are healthy, but Silver has HIGH RATIO with low absolute CLV — volume matters. Cannot ignore the Silver base.
  • Flaws: (1) Reward concentration at top creates "second-class" feel for Silver. (2) No clear path/progression visible to Silver (which would motivate upgrading rather than churning).
  • Redesign: (1) Introduce "next-tier preview" — give Silver members 1 free perk per quarter normally available to Gold to motivate aspiration. (2) Personalised offers (birthday miles, route-based suggestions). Earning rule: 1 mile per ₹1 spent + 2x miles for repeat routes. AI-driven offer personalisation based on travel patterns.
