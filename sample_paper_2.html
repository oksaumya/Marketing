SECTION C — Case-Based Application (2 × 9 = 18 Marks)

Both compulsory. Structure with headings. ~1 page each.

[9 Marks] Q9. Caselet 1 — The Beauty Brand at the Crossroads
Context: "GlowEdit" is a 3-year-old Indian D2C skincare startup that started selling exclusively on its website and Instagram. It has 200,000 customers, average order value ₹850, and strong unit economics.

To grow further, the founders are debating four options:
  • A: Open 20 exclusive brand outlets in Tier-1 metros.
  • B: List on Nykaa, Amazon, and Flipkart (modern + online retail).
  • C: Launch a premium anti-ageing range priced 3x current SKUs.
  • D: Expand into men's grooming under the same brand name.
  1. Place each of the four options (A, B, C, D) on the Ansoff Matrix with justification. Draw the matrix. (4 marks)
  2. Identify two channel-conflict risks that option B (listing on marketplaces) could create with the existing D2C website. (2 marks)
  3. If forced to choose only one option, which would you recommend and why? Tie your answer to STP and brand positioning. (3 marks)
OR
[9 Marks] Q9 (Alt). Caselet 1 (Alternative) — The Failed Launch
Context: A leading FMCG company launches a "healthy oats biscuit" priced at ₹40 per pack (regular biscuits sell at ₹10–15). They advertise heavily on prime-time TV, place it on premium shelves in modern retail only, and use a young Bollywood actor as endorser.

After 6 months: low repeat purchases, high returns from retailers, and the product is killed.
  1. Conduct a 4Ps diagnosis of why this product failed. Identify the weakest P and at least two misalignments. (4 marks)
  2. Apply the "Crossing the Chasm" concept to this case — which adopter segment did they target and where might they have gone wrong? (2 marks)
  3. Suggest a relaunch strategy using a revised 4P mix. (3 marks)
[9 Marks] Q10. Caselet 2 — Reviving Loyalty at "MyGrocer"
Context: "MyGrocer" is a regional online grocery platform competing with BigBasket and JioMart. They have 1.5 million customers, but:
  • Churn rate is 22% annually (industry average is 12%).
  • Their existing "MyPoints" program gives 1 point per ₹100 — but redemption rate is only 8%.
  • 60% of their churned customers cite "lack of new content/offers" and "delivery delays".
  • CAC is ₹450 per customer; ARPU is ₹2,800/year; gross margin is 15%.
  1. Calculate the approximate CLV assuming an average customer stays 3 years. Comment on the CLV:CAC ratio. (3 marks)
  2. Diagnose three reasons why the current loyalty program is failing and recommend a redesigned loyalty program (specify type + earning + redemption + one personalisation feature). (3 marks)
  3. Design a brief IMC plan with at least three coordinated touchpoints (advertising, sales promotion, digital, PR — any 3) to reduce churn and re-engage customers. (3 marks)
OR
[9 Marks] Q10 (Alt). Caselet 2 (Alternative) — The MLM Controversy
Context: "VitaPlus", a wellness MLM company operating in India, has grown from 5,000 distributors to 50,000 in two years. Recently, news channels have reported that 70% of distributors earn less than ₹2,000/month, and the top 1% earn over 80% of total commissions.

Existing distributors are encouraged primarily to recruit new distributors. The product is a multivitamin sold for ₹2,500/bottle, while competitor products are available at ₹400.
  1. Evaluate whether VitaPlus is operating closer to a legitimate MLM or a pyramid scheme. Cite at least three specific points from the case. (3 marks)
  2. Discuss the ethical concerns in this scenario from a marketing perspective. Reference India's Direct Selling Rules. (3 marks)
  3. If VitaPlus genuinely believes in its product, recommend an alternative go-to-market strategy (other than MLM) that they could pivot to. Justify with 4Ps. (3 marks)

Section A

Q1. Core (communication), Actual (phone/camera/OS), Augmented (warranty, EMI, app store). Augmented is hardest to copy — it's an ecosystem, services, brand promise.

Q2. Micro = close, somewhat controllable (suppliers, customers, retailers). Macro = broad, uncontrollable (PESTEL factors).

Q3. Laptop = Complex Buying · Salt = Habitual · Chips = Variety-Seeking · Insurance = Dissonance-Reducing.

Q4. Cart abandonment = customers add to cart but don't complete checkout. Reasons: unexpected costs, complicated checkout, security concerns. Recovery: email retargeting (10–15%), scarcity/urgency triggers.

Q5. CLV = AOV × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan. 3:1 ratio means each ₹1 of acquisition produces ₹3 of lifetime value — healthy margin for sustainable growth.

Section B

Q6. (a) Penetration pricing — price-sensitive student segment, need to build share fast, scale economies later. (b) Selective distribution via dealerships in college towns + online channel — students are concentrated, no need for intensive coverage.

Q7. Promotions create short-term sales spike but cause deal addiction, brand dilution, reduced willingness to pay full price. Example: fashion e-commerce brands always on 70% sale → customers wait for sales, full-price sales drop. Heavy discounting devalues brand equity.

Q8. LIC = Remind (Maturity) · UPI 2016 = Inform (Introduction) · Coke festive = Remind (Maturity) · Pepsi vs Coke = Persuade (Growth/Maturity competitive).

Section C — Caselet Skeletons

Q9 (GlowEdit):

  • Ansoff — A: Market Penetration (same products, same market, new channel). B: Market Penetration (existing products, wider reach, same target). C: Product Development (new range, same target). D: Diversification (new market segment + new positioning for men).
  • Channel conflict risks of B — Price undercutting (Nykaa flash sales hurt D2C), brand dilution (placement next to mass brands), loss of customer data (marketplace owns the relationship).
  • Recommended: B (modern retail listing) — fastest growth without diluting brand if executed with channel-specific SKUs and consistent MRPs. Aligns with STP (mass-affordable skincare for young women in metros).

Q9 (Alt — Healthy Biscuit): Weakest P = Price (3–4x normal biscuits without proven value perception). Misalignments: premium retail-only Place excludes mass biscuit buyers; TV ads have broad reach but Place doesn't match. Crossing the Chasm — targeted mainstream (Early Majority) before winning Early Adopters of health food. Relaunch: re-position as "premium snacking" at ₹25, distribute in modern + kirana, partner with fitness influencers.

Q10 (MyGrocer):

  • CLV ≈ ₹2,800 × 15% × 3 = ₹1,260. CLV:CAC ratio = 1,260 : 450 ≈ 2.8 : 1 — borderline; needs improvement.
  • Loyalty failures: (1) Earning too slow — 1 point per ₹100 is not motivating. (2) Redemption barriers (low 8% rate). (3) No personalisation. Redesign: Tiered (Silver/Gold/Platinum) + free delivery for higher tiers + personalised offers based on past purchases (e.g., milk-buyers get dairy-subscription discount).
  • IMC: WhatsApp personalised offer + paid Instagram retargeting + in-app "Welcome Back" offer + email — all carrying the same "Your favourites at the prices you love" message.

Q10 (Alt — VitaPlus):

  • Closer to pyramid — recruitment dominates compensation, earnings concentrated at top, product priced 6x competitors (suggests product is token), most distributors lose money.
  • Ethical issues: misleading income claims, exploiting social relationships, overpriced product. India's Direct Selling Rules (2016/Consumer Protection 2021) require: genuine sales, buy-back guarantee, no money circulation, disclosure of realistic earnings.
  • Pivot to D2C + retail: Product (re-formulate at competitive price), Price (₹500–800 vs current ₹2,500), Place (Amazon + chemists + own website), Promotion (digital + influencer marketing, transparent claims).
