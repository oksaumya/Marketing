1 2 Marks Eco-friendly notebooks: students prefer stylish over plain recycled Identify the core marketing concept reflected and justify.

Concept: This reflects the Marketing Concept / Customer Orientation — and specifically the distinction between a Need and a Want.

Justification: The basic need is for a notebook to write on. However, students have transformed this into a want for stylish, attractive notebooks shaped by their preferences and self-image. The startup must focus on what customers actually want (style + eco-friendliness), not just what the producer thinks is good (plain recycled). Marketing succeeds when it satisfies the customer's want, not just the basic need.

2 2 Marks Food app studies customer ordering patterns for midnight delivery Which consumer behaviour factor is being analysed?

Factor: Behavioural Factor (Usage / Purchase Patterns).

Explanation: The app is examining when, what, and how often customers order — these are behavioural variables in consumer behaviour analysis. By understanding patterns like late-night cravings, the company can identify a gap and launch a midnight delivery service that matches actual consumer habits.

3 2 Marks Smartphone price reduction during festive season Identify the pricing strategy and state one benefit.

Strategy: Promotional / Discount Pricing (Sales Promotion Pricing) — a short-term price reduction tied to a festive occasion.

Benefit: It boosts sales volume by attracting price-sensitive buyers, helps clear inventory, and creates urgency among customers to purchase during the festive period. Additionally, it can drive new customer acquisition.

4 2 Marks Cosmetics dividing customers into teenagers, working professionals, senior citizens Which STP element is applied?

STP Element: Segmentation — specifically Demographic Segmentation based on Age.

Explanation: The company is dividing its market into smaller homogeneous groups so that it can design tailored advertisements for each age group. Teenagers, professionals and seniors have very different skincare needs, communication preferences and purchasing power — hence age-based segmentation enables more effective marketing.

5 2 Marks Handmade products promoted through Instagram influencers, no physical store Which element of the marketing mix is highlighted?

Element: Promotion (specifically Digital / Influencer Marketing).

Explanation: The entrepreneur is communicating product value to customers through paid social media collaborations instead of using traditional retail or advertising. This is a modern form of the Promotion P — leveraging influencer reach, credibility and authenticity to create awareness, interest and demand for handmade products.

PART – B  |  Answer all Questions (4 × 3 = 12 Marks)
6 3 Marks Rural entrepreneur — organic snacks in Haryana villages Explain any three macro-environmental factors influencing success.

The macro environment consists of broad, uncontrollable forces (PESTEL). Three relevant factors:

  1. Economic: Rising rural disposable income and increasing spending on packaged/health food. Inflation may affect raw material cost. The entrepreneur must price snacks affordably for villagers.
  2. Social / Cultural: Growing health-consciousness even in rural Haryana; preference for traditional ingredients. Local taste preferences (e.g., spice levels) must be respected in product formulation.
  3. Technological: Penetration of smartphones, UPI, and JioMart/local delivery apps allows direct marketing & cashless payment. The entrepreneur can use WhatsApp marketing and digital payments to reach customers.

(Other valid factors: Political — government schemes for rural MSMEs; Legal — FSSAI certification; Environmental — climate affecting agriculture supply.)

7 3 Marks Fashion startup — sales declined after 2 years of rapid growth (a) Identify PLC stage. (b) Suggest two strategies.

(a) PLC Stage: Decline Stage — characterised by falling sales after a period of growth and maturity. This often happens in fashion due to changing trends, new competitors, and saturation.

(b) Two Suitable Strategies:

  1. Re-launch / Reposition the Product: Introduce new designs, colours, or sustainable variants to attract a new segment. Example: Re-position towards Gen Z with eco-conscious messaging.
  2. Diversify into New Markets or Categories: Use the Ansoff Matrix's Market Development (enter Tier-2/3 cities, export markets) or Product Development (launch accessories/related lines) to revive revenue.

(Alternative: harvesting strategy — minimise cost, milk remaining profits before exit.)

8 3 Marks Mobile company — premium smartwatch for fitness consumers Explain how STP can help the company succeed.

Apply STP as follows:

  1. Segmentation: Divide the smartwatch market on multiple bases — Demographic (urban, 20–40 yrs, mid-to-high income), Psychographic (fitness-oriented, health-conscious lifestyle), Behavioural (gym-goers, runners, athletes).
  2. Targeting: Choose the segment of fitness-conscious urban professionals with disposable income — a focused niche where willingness to pay is high.
  3. Positioning: Establish a distinct image — "The premium fitness companion that helps you achieve your health goals." Emphasise advanced health-tracking features, design, and battery life. Differentiate from cheaper smartwatches and general-purpose ones.

STP ensures the right product reaches the right customer with the right message, maximising acceptance and avoiding wasted marketing spend.

9 3 Marks Supermarket loyalty program (points per purchase) Explain any three benefits of loyalty programs.
  1. Customer Retention: Rewards make customers return to redeem points, reducing churn. Retention is 5× cheaper than acquisition — directly improving profitability.
  2. Higher Customer Lifetime Value (CLV): Loyal customers buy more frequently and spend more per visit. A 5% increase in retention can boost profits by 25–95%.
  3. Customer Data & Personalisation: Each transaction provides behavioural data. The supermarket can send personalised offers (e.g., dairy discounts to milk-buyers), enhancing relevance and conversion.

(Other benefits: word-of-mouth advocacy, competitive differentiation, customer insights, upselling/cross-selling.)

10 9 Marks Case Study — Amul's Rural Market Expansion
Context Recap: Amul introduced smaller, affordable milk packs in rural India, strengthened distribution through local retailers and ran village-level promotional campaigns, leading to higher penetration in semi-urban & rural areas.

(a) Two Elements of the Marketing Mix used by Amul (4 Marks):

  1. Product: Amul redesigned its product portfolio for rural markets by introducing smaller and affordable pack sizes. This is product adaptation — recognising that rural consumers buy in smaller quantities due to lower per-capita income, no refrigeration, and daily consumption patterns. By making the product accessible in suitable sizes, Amul matched the actual usage and affordability behaviour of rural households.
  2. Place (Distribution): Amul built a strong network using local retailers and village-level distributors. This intensive rural distribution strategy created place utility — making the product available even in remote semi-urban & rural areas where modern retail does not reach. It also leveraged trust in local retailers, ensuring better customer reach and brand acceptance.

(Bonus elements: Price — affordable pricing for middle-income; Promotion — village-level local campaigns.)

(b) How Distribution Strategies Helped Improve Market Penetration (5 Marks):

  1. Last-Mile Reach: By appointing village-level retailers and small kirana partners, Amul ensured that its products were physically available within walking distance for rural consumers — overcoming the geographic barrier.
  2. Leveraging Intermediaries: Local retailers acted as trusted intermediaries who already had customer relationships. They performed key functions — bulk-breaking, storage, credit, and personal recommendation — at a much lower cost than Amul setting up its own stores.
  3. Cold-Chain & Logistical Network: Amul invested in cooperative cold-chain infrastructure linking farmer producers, processing plants and distributors. This ensured freshness and consistent supply — critical for a perishable product like milk.
  4. Increased Frequency of Purchase: Easy availability + small packs encouraged daily/weekly buying, increasing penetration rate and average order frequency.
  5. Word-of-Mouth & Trust: Village-level promotions combined with the local retailer's endorsement built strong trust — a powerful driver in rural markets where peer recommendation matters more than advertising.
Conclusion: Amul's distribution depth + product adaptation enabled it to convert "non-consumers" into "regular consumers", leading to deep rural market penetration that competitors found hard to replicate.
11 9 Marks Case Study — Coca-Cola's Localized Marketing
Context Recap: Coca-Cola customised advertisements and packaging using regional festivals & languages; during summer it introduced localised slogans and affordable pricing to attract customers across India.

(a) How Localisation supports Coca-Cola's Marketing Strategy in India (4 Marks):

  1. Cultural Relevance: India is highly diverse — language, festivals, food habits vary across states. By customising messaging to regional festivals (Onam, Pongal, Durga Puja, Diwali), Coca-Cola appears as a brand that belongs to each region rather than a foreign import.
  2. Emotional Connect through Language: Using local languages (Tamil, Hindi, Bengali, Marathi etc.) in slogans and packaging makes the message instantly understandable and emotionally resonant. This boosts brand recall and trust.
  3. Targeting Diverse Segments: Different regions have different climates, tastes and purchasing power. Localisation allows Coca-Cola to tailor pricing, pack sizes and flavours (e.g., Maaza for mango-loving North India).
  4. Stronger Brand Equity: Localisation positions Coca-Cola as a brand that respects Indian culture — building long-term brand loyalty and consumer goodwill.

(b) Importance of Pricing and Promotion in Seasonal Customer Engagement (5 Marks):

LeverRole in Seasonal Engagement
Affordable PricingLower prices during summer (mass-pack offers, ₹10 small bottles) make the product accessible to a wide income range — turning aspiration into actual purchase.
Bundling & Festival CombosSpecial festive multi-pack pricing increases perceived value and encourages bulk buying during weddings, celebrations, family gatherings.
Promotional Campaigns ("Thanda Matlab Coca-Cola", "Open Happiness")Memorable slogans tied to occasion (heat, festival) create immediate desire and brand recall. The campaign drives emotional engagement.
Sales PromotionsLimited-period offers, scratch cards, "Buy 2 Get 1" boost short-term consumption and create urgency during festivals/summer.
Channel & POS PushEye-catching displays, branded fridges in retail outlets during peak season convert footfall into purchase.
Conclusion: Pricing makes the product accessible; promotion makes the product desirable. Together, in a seasonal context, they create a sharp spike in customer engagement, footfall and consumption — turning a regular beverage into part of the cultural celebration.
