PART – A  |  Answer all Questions (5 × 2 = 10 Marks)
1 2 Marks Restaurant gives free birthday dessert from sign-up data Identify the marketing concept and justify.

Concept: Customer Relationship Management (CRM) / Relationship Marketing — supported by personalisation through customer data.

Justification: The restaurant uses sign-up data (birthday, preferences) to build a long-term, personal relationship with each customer. Offering a free dessert on the birthday creates an emotional connection, drives repeat visits, builds loyalty and increases Customer Lifetime Value — all core CRM objectives.

2 2 Marks Laptop launched at very high price, reduced gradually over a year Identify the pricing strategy.

Strategy: Skimming Pricing (Price Skimming).

Explanation: The brand sets a high initial price to "skim" the maximum profit from early adopters and tech enthusiasts who are less price-sensitive. The price is gradually reduced over time to attract more cost-conscious segments. This strategy is typical for innovative technology products (e.g., iPhone launches) and helps recover R&D costs quickly.

3 2 Marks Customers buy detergent repeatedly without comparing brands Type of buying behaviour (Assael's framework)?

Type: Habitual Buying Behaviour.

Justification: In Assael's matrix, habitual buying occurs when involvement is low AND brand differences are few. Detergent is a frequently purchased, low-cost FMCG item. Customers buy out of routine, brand familiarity and convenience — they neither research nor compare. Marketing focus: availability + reminder advertising.

4 2 Marks Premium watch brand sells only through few authorised showrooms Which distribution intensity strategy?

Strategy: Exclusive Distribution.

Justification: Only a very small number of authorised outlets are permitted to sell the product. This is used for high-involvement, premium & luxury brands (Rolex, Omega) to maintain brand image, control customer experience, ensure premium positioning, and avoid brand dilution that mass distribution would cause.

5 2 Marks Skincare brand designs packaging in Hindi, Tamil, Bengali for different states Which segmentation base?

Base: Geographic Segmentation (also acceptable: cultural / linguistic segmentation).

Explanation: The brand is dividing the market by region/state and adapting packaging language accordingly. Different geographic regions of India have different dominant languages and cultural preferences, so this approach increases local relevance, comprehension and trust among regional consumers.

PART – B  |  Answer all Questions (4 × 3 = 12 Marks)
6 3 Marks Small Delhi bakery expanding across NCR Explain three micro-environmental factors affecting expansion.

Micro factors are close to the firm and partially controllable. Three relevant ones:

  1. Customers: NCR has diverse preferences — Delhi NCR's young professionals may want artisanal/healthy bakes, while older customers prefer traditional sweets. The bakery must adapt its product range.
  2. Competitors: Strong presence of chains (Theobroma, Wengers, L'Opera) + local bakeries in every neighbourhood. Differentiation through pricing, niche product or experience is critical.
  3. Suppliers: Reliable, hygienic supply of flour, butter, dairy is essential. Any disruption (delays, price hikes) directly affects the bakery's output and quality.

(Other factors: Intermediaries — Swiggy/Zomato delivery partners; Public — local food bloggers, reviewers.)

7 3 Marks Health drink — 10 years old, sales flat with strong profits (a) Identify PLC stage. (b) Suggest two strategies.

(a) PLC Stage: Maturity Stage — characterised by stable but flat sales, high profits, market saturation, intense competition, and slowing growth.

(b) Two Strategies:

  1. Product Modification: Introduce new flavours, healthier variants, or new packaging sizes to revive interest. (e.g., a sugar-free version, premium glass-bottle variant.)
  2. Market Modification / New Segments: Target new user groups (e.g., gym-goers, children, women) or geographic markets to grow volume. Use refreshed positioning and Promotion mix.
8 3 Marks Food delivery startup entering Tier-3 cities Explain how 5-stage Consumer Decision Process can guide marketing strategy.
StageMarketing Action for Tier-3 entry
1. Need RecognitionCreate awareness that food delivery exists — use local radio, vernacular ads, banners in marketplaces.
2. Information SearchRun WhatsApp campaigns, local influencer videos, and demos in regional language to educate users.
3. Evaluation of AlternativesHighlight differentiators — speed, COD, regional restaurants, lower delivery charge vs going out.
4. Purchase DecisionUse first-order discounts, free delivery, and easy UPI/COD options to reduce friction.
5. Post-PurchaseEnsure on-time delivery, friendly support, follow-up offers — convert first-time users into repeat customers.

Mapping each stage to specific marketing actions ensures no leakage in the conversion funnel.

9 3 Marks Streaming platform — Basic / Standard / Premium tiers Three benefits of tiered loyalty / subscription model.
  1. Customer Retention & Upselling: Customers start with Basic and progressively upgrade to Standard/Premium — reducing churn and increasing CLV.
  2. Customer Segmentation: Each tier represents a different segment (price-sensitive vs feature-seeking vs premium). The company can serve all without alienating any.
  3. Higher Revenue per User (ARPU): Tier differentiation captures more value from heavy users (HD, 4K, multiple screens) while still attracting new users via Basic.

(Other benefits: better data collection, brand aspiration through Premium tier, predictable recurring revenue.)

10 9 Marks Case Study — Patanjali's Entry into FMCG
Context Recap: Patanjali entered FMCG positioning as swadeshi, Ayurvedic and affordable, used Baba Ramdev as brand ambassador, priced 15–30% lower than MNCs, and distributed through modern + local retail. Within 5 years, it captured significant share in toothpaste, ghee & shampoo categories.

(a) Two Marketing Mix Elements (4 Marks):

  1. Price: Patanjali deliberately priced its products 15–30% lower than MNC competitors. This value-based + penetration pricing approach attracted middle-class Indian households and built rapid market share. Affordability became a key driver of adoption.
  2. Promotion: Patanjali leveraged Baba Ramdev as a credible, free brand ambassador with a massive yoga following. Combined with the "swadeshi" (made in India) narrative, this created emotional resonance and trust without traditional celebrity endorsement costs.

(b) STP Differentiation from MNCs (5 Marks):

  1. Segmentation:
    • Psychographic: Indians who believe in Ayurveda, natural ingredients, and swadeshi values.
    • Demographic: Middle-income households across urban & semi-urban India.
    • Behavioural: Health-conscious buyers, traditional medicine users.
  2. Targeting: A large under-served segment — patriotic, health-conscious, value-seeking middle-class Indians who felt MNCs were too expensive and lacked Indian authenticity. This was an attractive, mostly ignored mass-niche.
  3. Positioning: "Affordable, natural, Ayurvedic, Swadeshi alternative to MNC brands". This positioning is distinct, memorable, and rests on emotional pillars (national pride, tradition, trust) that MNCs cannot easily replicate.
Conclusion: By clearly identifying and serving an under-served segment with a unique positioning, Patanjali differentiated itself sharply — forcing MNCs like HUL and Colgate to launch their own herbal/Ayurvedic ranges in response.
11 9 Marks Case Study — Zomato's Customer Retention
Context Recap: Zomato Gold offers free delivery on orders > ₹199, additional discounts at partner restaurants, gamified rewards. Uses past-order data for personalisation. Gold member repeat-order rate has risen significantly.

(a) Type of Loyalty Programme + Two Benefits (4 Marks):

Type: Paid Subscription Loyalty Programme (with elements of gamification). Customers pay an annual/monthly fee for exclusive perks.

Two Customer Benefits:

  1. Free Delivery + Exclusive Discounts: Members save money on every order (free delivery above ₹199, up to 30% off at partner restaurants). Subscription quickly pays for itself for frequent users.
  2. Gamified Rewards & Perks: Earn badges, predict-the-score challenges, and surprise perks make the experience engaging and fun — going beyond a transactional reward.

(b) Personalisation, Data and Customer Retention (5 Marks):

  1. Better Recommendations: Zomato analyses past orders to suggest similar cuisines/restaurants, reducing search effort and increasing order frequency.
  2. Targeted Offers: A customer who orders biryani every weekend can receive personalised biryani-restaurant offers, raising relevance & conversion.
  3. Reduced Churn: Personalised experience makes switching to competitors (Swiggy) less attractive — users invest emotionally in their Zomato profile.
  4. Higher Customer Lifetime Value (CLV): Increased order frequency × longer retention × higher AOV = greater CLV. CLV = AOV × Purchase Frequency × Lifespan.
  5. Data Network Effect: Each interaction adds more data, making recommendations smarter — a self-reinforcing loop that competitors find hard to break.
Conclusion: Personalisation + paid loyalty creates a "double lock-in" — emotional + financial — improving retention and profitability significantly.
12 9 Marks Case Study — Mamaearth's D2C Growth
Context Recap: Mamaearth started D2C (own website + Instagram), focused on toxin-free natural skincare, used influencer collaborations. Later entered modern retail (Nykaa, Amazon) and physical stores — became a unicorn.

(a) Two Promotional Strategies + Importance (4 Marks):

  1. Influencer Marketing: Mamaearth partnered with hundreds of micro-influencers and content creators on Instagram & YouTube. This created authentic word-of-mouth, especially among young women — credible & cheaper than celebrities, with much higher engagement rates.
  2. Content-Led Digital Storytelling: The brand consistently used the "toxin-free, made-safe" narrative across all touchpoints — social posts, packaging, ads — building trust among health-conscious mothers and Gen Z. Strong storytelling generated emotional buy-in.

(b) D2C to Omnichannel — Advantages & Challenges (5 Marks):

AdvantagesChallenges
Wider Reach: Modern retail puts products in front of millions who don't shop online.Channel Conflict: Lower D2C website prices vs marketplace MRP cause friction.
Offline Trust: Skin products often need to be felt/tested — physical stores help.Margin Pressure: Retailers take 20–35% margin, squeezing profit.
Multiple Touchpoints: Customer can discover on Instagram, research on website, buy on Amazon, sample at Nykaa store — seamless journey.Inventory Sync: Coordinating stock across channels is operationally complex.
Brand Validation: Being on Nykaa shelves signals legitimacy.Loss of First-Party Data: Marketplaces own the customer relationship & data.
Higher Volume: Scale economies in production, marketing & logistics.Brand Consistency Risk: Different channels may dilute the brand promise if not coordinated via IMC.
Conclusion: Omnichannel is essential for scale but requires strict pricing discipline, inventory sync, consistent IMC, and protection of D2C profitability — Mamaearth has executed this well.
